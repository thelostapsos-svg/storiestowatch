What it is

The Oscars of watchmaking

One independent jury, 14 categories, and a gold hand called the Aiguille d’Or.

The GPHG has run since 2001 as the industry’s most credible, brand-neutral awards. Makers enter their best new work; an academy nominates six watches per category; and a jury picks the winners of 19 prizes, crowned by the “Aiguille d’Or” Grand Prix — the golden hand, watchmaking’s best-in-show. This year the jury is chaired for the first time by Wei Koh, founder of Revolution (Surface).

Crucially, the GPHG isn’t a rich-list. Its categories run from Time Only and Petite Aiguille (watches under a few thousand francs) all the way to Tourbillon, Mechanical Exception and Calendar & Astronomy. A tiny independent can beat a giant. That is exactly why it’s the truest read of talent in the room — the 2026 categories in full below.

The standouts

The record-breakers in the room

Two nominees tell you how far the outer edge of watchmaking has moved.

Every GPHG has its jaw-droppers, and 2026 is no exception. Konstantin Chaykin’s ThinKing Mystery is billed as the world’s thinnest series-produced mechanical watch, at 1.65mm — thinner than many coins. At the other pole of ambition, Christiaan van der Klaauw’s Grand Planetarium carries a working model of the solar system on the wrist, tracking all eight planets in real time (Surface).

Between those two extremes sit the watches most collectors will actually chase: the year’s best chronographs, the integrated-steel sports pieces, the calendars and the dress classics. The GPHG is where a reputation is made — a nomination alone can move a small maker’s waitlist overnight, and an Aiguille d’Or can define a brand for a decade.

Why it matters — and where to see it

The tour stops in Mumbai

Before Geneva crowns the winners, the nominees come to you — including India.

The GPHG matters to a buyer for a simple reason: it’s a curated shortlist of the best new watches on earth, chosen by people with no product to sell you. Read the winners on 7 November and you have a ready-made map of what’s worth wanting — and, one tier down, which references are quietly appreciating, the theme we track in the 2026 market read.

And this year the show travels. The nominated watches go on a world tour — Mumbai, Bangkok, Geneva and London — before all 84 are exhibited at Geneva’s Musée d’Art et d’Histoire from 29 October to 15 November, with the ceremony on 7 November (GPHG). For our many readers in India, a Mumbai stop is a rare chance to see this calibre of watchmaking in the metal.

Questions, answered

The quick answers

What is the GPHG?

The Grand Prix d’Horlogerie de Genève is the most prestigious awards in watchmaking — often called the “Oscars of watchmaking.” Running since 2001, it is brand-neutral: an academy and an independent jury nominate and reward the year’s best watches across categories, and the top prize is the “Aiguille d’Or” Grand Prix.

How many watches are nominated for GPHG 2026?

84 timepieces are nominated for GPHG 2026, six in each of 14 categories, selected from 320 watches entered by 194 brands. The nominees were announced on 15 September 2026, and 19 prizes will be awarded at the ceremony in Geneva on 7 November 2026.

When are the GPHG 2026 winners announced?

The winners are revealed at the awards ceremony at the Bâtiment des Forces Motrices in Geneva on 7 November 2026. Before that, the nominated watches tour Mumbai, Bangkok, Geneva and London, and are exhibited together at Geneva’s Musée d’Art et d’Histoire from 29 October to 15 November.

What are the standout GPHG 2026 nominees?

Two extremes drew the most attention: Konstantin Chaykin’s ThinKing Mystery, billed as the world’s thinnest series-produced mechanical watch at 1.65mm, and Christiaan van der Klaauw’s Grand Planetarium, which tracks all eight planets in real time on the wrist. The 2026 jury is chaired for the first time by Wei Koh, founder of Revolution.
