On 9 October 2026, a stainless-steel Voutilainen 28Sport sold for $1,016,000 at Phillips’ online sale The New York Sessions, Fall 2026. Phillips had estimated it at $120,000–240,000, so the final price was about 8.5 times the low estimate. The watch is No. 5 of only 8 made in steel, and Phillips says it is “possibly one of only two” with this onyx-centre dial. It came from its original owner as a full set. The watch has no gold, no gems and no complication beyond small seconds. Bidders paid for the name on the dial and for the fact that so few exist. HauteTime reports that all 88 lots in the sale sold. Even so, a steel time-only watch from a one-man maison was the lot that crossed a million.

What happened

How a $120,000 estimate became $1,016,000

The watch led the sale before it opened, and then it outran its estimate by a wide margin.

The Voutilainen 28Sport was Lot 22 in Phillips’ online New York sale, held with Bacs & Russo. The sale ran 1–9 October 2026, and its 88 lots closed one minute apart from 10am ET on the last day. Pre-sale coverage already named the 28Sport as the lead lot. During the week, reported bids reached $550,000 and then $700,000. When the lot closed, Phillips’ own lot page recorded the final price as $1,016,000 including buyer’s premium.

Phillips’ catalogue describes the watch as follows. It has a 39mm steel case with teardrop lugs. The manual-wind Calibre 28 (movement no. 28-272) has a large balance wheel and a Phillips overcoil. The dial combines a blue guilloché hour track with a centre and small-seconds register in glossy black onyx. It came with a Voutilainen certificate dated October 2021, a presentation box and an extra strap. In short, the watch is rare, complete and comes from its first owner, which is the kind of lot top collectors compete hardest for.

The watch

What is the Voutilainen 28Sport?

Kari Voutilainen made a sports watch in very small numbers.

Kari Voutilainen is a Finnish watchmaker based in Môtiers, Switzerland. He is best known for dress watches with hand-guillochéd dials. In 2020 he released the 28Sport, his take on a sports watch. Phillips records it as two limited series of eight pieces each, one in Grade 5 titanium and one in stainless steel, both 39mm. That makes 16 watches across the two metals. The 28Sport uses the same Calibre 28 found in his dress watches, which has two escape wheels that give direct impulse to the balance. It comes on a hand-stitched textile strap, not an integrated bracelet.

The rest of his output is just as small. Phillips reported in June 2026 that the manufacture makes fewer than 60 watches a year. The same report said the brand had stopped taking new orders to shorten waiting lists that had grown to more than a decade. For a buyer today, that means the secondary market is effectively the only way to get one. When a steel example with a rare dial comes from its first owner, bidders know another may not appear for years. In the market as we read it across the 75 verified dealers in our Watch Finder, we can usually find a Nautilus or a Daytona at some price. A Voutilainen in open stock is very rare.

The sale in numbers

Independents vs. the big names, in a single sale

Several watches in this sale went well past their estimates, and all of them came from independent makers.

The sale lets us compare independents and blue chips directly. The same bidders, on the same days, had both kinds of watch in front of them. The independent pieces sold for several times their low estimates. The Patek Philippe and the Rolex sold within theirs.

Why it matters

Are independent watchmakers the new blue chips?

At the top of the market, the results suggest yes. Lower down, the picture is more mixed.

This sale continues a trend we have tracked all year. In June 2026, an F.P. Journe Chronomètre à Résonance sold for $13,922,000 at Phillips New York. That is a world record for any independent watchmaker, and we covered it in our story on the $13.9M Journe. Four months later, a steel Voutilainen crossing $1 million shows that the demand reaches past one famous name and past precious metals. Collectors are paying for authorship (a single maker’s name and hand), for tiny production and for waiting lists they cannot join.

Most of the market does not look like this. Our 2026 market read describes a split. Scarce, story-rich pieces and the independents keep rising, while many mainstream references have softened. This sale fits that pattern. A modern gold Patek perpetual chronograph and a platinum Daytona sold for sensible prices within their estimates. The independents drew the competitive bidding. For the next test, the November Geneva sales are coming (see our Geneva auction-week preview), and Phillips Geneva XXIV opens on 7 November.

What it means for you

How to buy a Voutilainen, or a watch like one

With independents, the hard part is getting access, not finding the money.

You can’t simply walk into a shop and buy a Voutilainen. Phillips reports that the order book is closed and output is under 60 pieces a year, so nearly every purchase happens on the secondary market. That means the major auction houses and a small group of specialist dealers. What protects your money is the same at every level. Look for a full set with the original certificate, clear provenance, and a correct, unpolished case and dial. With hand-finished watches, a poor service or a refinished dial can take a large share off the value.

Don’t treat a headline price as a guide to value either. A $1M result is one evening with two determined bidders. It is not the market rate for every 28Sport, and much less for every Voutilainen. Before you bid on an independent, get an honest market read on that exact piece. Then decide whether you want the watch itself or just a share of the excitement around it. Questions, answered

The quick answers

How much did the Voutilainen 28Sport sell for?

A stainless-steel Voutilainen 28Sport, No. 5 of 8, sold for $1,016,000 including buyer’s premium at Phillips’ online sale The New York Sessions, Fall 2026, which closed on 9 October 2026. Its estimate was $120,000–240,000, so it sold for about 8.5 times the low estimate.

How many Voutilainen 28Sport watches were made?

Phillips records the 28Sport, launched in 2020, as two limited series of eight pieces each: eight in Grade 5 titanium and eight in stainless steel, all 39mm. The steel example sold in October 2026 is believed to be one of only two with an onyx-centre dial.

Why are Voutilainen watches so expensive?

Kari Voutilainen’s workshop makes fewer than 60 watches a year, with hand-made guilloché dials and in-house movements. Phillips reported in June 2026 that the brand had stopped taking new orders because waiting lists had passed a decade. Very small supply, the authorship of a single maker and a closed order book push prices on the secondary market far above original retail.

Are independent watches a good investment?

At the very top, independents such as F.P. Journe and Voutilainen have recently outperformed blue-chip brands at auction, including a record $13,922,000 Journe in June 2026. Results are concentrated in the rarest, best-documented pieces, though, and a single auction result is not a market price. Buy the watch you want, verify its provenance and condition, and treat any gain as a bonus.
