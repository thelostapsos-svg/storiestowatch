They are the three most wanted steel sports watches on earth, and collectors argue about them endlessly. But "which holds value best" has no single answer — because each one wins for a different kind of buyer. Here's how the Rolex Daytona, the Audemars Piguet Royal Oak and the Patek Philippe Nautilus actually compare in 2026: what they cost, how they've held, and which belongs on your wrist.

The contender

Rolex Daytona

The liquid one — the grail you can actually live with.

The steel Daytona (ref. 126500LN) is the most tradable watch in this trio. Rolex's scale means there's a deep, transparent market for it — you can buy one, wear it hard, and sell it in a week at a known number. It carries a strong premium over its ~$15,000 retail, but it's a fraction of the Patek's cost, which makes it the natural first grail for most collectors.

It's also the most wearable: 40mm, screw-down everything, genuinely robust. The trade-off is availability at retail — the waitlist is theoretical for most, so nearly everyone buys on the secondary market. See the live Rolex dealers in the Watch Finder.

The contender

Audemars Piguet Royal Oak

The purist's choice — Gérald Genta's original 1972 shock.

The Royal Oak is the watch that invented the luxury-steel-sports category. The current 15500ST (41mm) is the everyday hero; the discontinued 15202ST "Jumbo" is the connoisseur's true grail and trades at a serious multiple. Values held remarkably well through the 2022–24 correction because the design is untouchable and supply is genuinely limited.

It sits between the Daytona and the Nautilus on price and on ego: more exclusive than a Daytona, more attainable than a Nautilus, and — to a certain kind of collector — more beautiful than either. Full pricing in our Royal Oak price guide.

The contender

Patek Philippe Nautilus

The trophy — the highest ceiling, and the highest bar.

The Nautilus is the store-of-value king. The discontinued 5711/1A remains the single most valuable steel sports watch of the modern era, and even after the speculative froth of 2021–22 cooled, it trades far above everything else here. Its white-gold successor, the 5811/1G, carries the torch at the top.

But the ceiling comes with the highest bar: it's the hardest to buy, the most expensive by a wide margin, and the least "wearable" as an everyday piece precisely because of what it's worth. It's the trophy — not the daily. More in our Nautilus buying guide and investment guide.

The Nautilus wins on value, the Royal Oak on taste, the Daytona on livability. The "best" grail is simply the one whose trade-off is yours.

The verdict

So which should you buy?

Buy the Daytona if you want one grail you'll actually wear and can sell in a heartbeat — the safest, most liquid entry into this world.

Buy the Royal Oak if the object matters more than the flex — the connoisseur's pick, and arguably the most important design of the three.

Buy the Nautilus if you're buying the trophy and the store of value, and the price of entry isn't the point.

Whichever way you lean, the hard part isn't the choice — it's finding the right example at a fair number, from someone worth trusting. That's exactly what our concierge does.

Questions, answered

The quick answers

Which holds its value best — Royal Oak, Nautilus or Daytona?
The Patek Philippe Nautilus holds the highest absolute value and premium of the three, with the discontinued 5711/1A still the most valuable modern steel sports watch. The Royal Oak holds strongly and is more attainable, while the Rolex Daytona holds a solid premium and is by far the most liquid to buy and sell.
Which is the best first grail?
The Rolex Daytona. It's the most liquid and wearable of the three, sits at the lowest price of entry, and can be sold quickly at a known number — which makes it the safest way into the steel-grail world.
Why is the Nautilus so much more expensive?
Extreme scarcity and demand. Patek Philippe makes a tiny number of watches, the steel 5711 was discontinued in 2021, and it became the defining status watch of its era — so it trades far above the Royal Oak and Daytona even after the 2022 market cooled.
Can you actually buy these at retail?
Rarely. All three are effectively allocation-only or discontinued, so the vast majority of buyers purchase on the secondary market. That's why a verified source and an honest price matter more than a boutique waitlist.
