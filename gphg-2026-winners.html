On 7 November 2026, the Grand Prix d’Horlogerie de Genève — watchmaking’s Oscars — handed out its twenty prizes in Geneva, and the biggest of them all, the Aiguille d’Or Grand Prix, went to Rexhep Rexhepi for his Chronograph Flyback: his first chronograph, and the first complication to carry his own name. Not a great Swiss house with a marketing army — a single independent watchmaker. And he wasn’t alone at the podium. Across the night, 2026 looked less like a parade of the big maisons than a coronation of the independents. Here is every winner, and what it all means.

What happened

Rexhep Rexhepi wins the Golden Hand

The top honour in watchmaking went to a one-man Geneva workshop.

The Aiguille d’Or (“Golden Hand”) is the GPHG’s highest award — the single best watch across all categories, chosen from the 84 nominees we previewed last month. In 2026 it went to the Rexhep Rexhepi Chronograph Flyback, the debut chronograph from the Geneva independent widely regarded as one of the finest watchmakers of his generation (Watchonista). For an independent to take the crown over Patek, Audemars Piguet and the rest is the story of the night — and of the year.

In all, 20 prizes were awarded. The great houses were far from shut out — Audemars Piguet took two, Chopard two, Patek Philippe a Special Jury Prize — but the shape of the results told its own tale.

The story

2026 was the year of the independents

The crown, and category after category, went to small workshops.

Read the list again and a pattern jumps out. The Aiguille d’Or went to an independent. So did the Tourbillon and Chronometry prizes (both Ferdinand Berthoud), the Essential (Daniel Roth), the Complication (Cléguer), the Audacity (Konstantin Chaykin), the Horological Revelation (Dominique Renaud), the Petite Aiguille (Japan’s Otsuka Lotec), the Mechanical Clock (La Vallée) and the Challenge (SpaceOne × Baltic). The great maisons took the glamour categories — Audemars Piguet’s Iconic and Calendar wins, Chopard’s Time Only and Jewellery, Blancpain’s Mechanical Exception, Louis Vuitton’s Artistic Crafts — but the soul of the evening sat with the small shops.

It is the same current we’ve watched all year: a steel Voutilainen that broke a million dollars at auction, F.P. Journe records stacking up, collectors chasing the handmade over the mass-produced. The GPHG just made it official. If you want to understand where the serious money and the serious taste are heading in watchmaking, the 2026 podium is the clearest map yet.

What it means for you

Can you actually own a GPHG winner?

Most are grails; a couple are genuinely attainable.

Honestly? Most of these are not watches you can walk in and buy. The independent winners — Rexhepi, Ferdinand Berthoud, Daniel Roth, Otsuka Lotec — are made in tiny numbers, often to order, with waiting lists measured in years; and the house grand-complications run deep into six and seven figures. These are trophies, not shopping-list items.

But the podium isn’t all unobtainable. The Sports winner, Grand Seiko’s Spring Drive diver, sits in genuinely reachable territory, and Chopard’s Time Only L.U.C is a real-world buy. Questions, answered

The quick answers

Who won the 2026 GPHG Aiguille d’Or?

Rexhep Rexhepi won the 2026 Aiguille d’Or Grand Prix — the top prize at the Grand Prix d’Horlogerie de Genève — for his Chronograph Flyback, his first chronograph and the first complication to carry his own name. The ceremony was held in Geneva on 7 November 2026.

What is the Aiguille d’Or Grand Prix?

The Aiguille d’Or, or “Golden Hand,” is the highest honour at the GPHG — often called watchmaking’s Oscars. It is awarded each year to the single most outstanding watch across all categories. In 2026 it went to the independent Geneva watchmaker Rexhep Rexhepi.

Who were the other major 2026 GPHG winners?

Among the twenty prizes, Audemars Piguet took the Iconic award (Royal Oak “Jumbo” RD#5) and Calendar & Astronomy (150 Heritage); Patek Philippe received the Special Jury Prize; Chopard won Time Only (L.U.C 1860) and Jewellery (Mystery Nut); Grand Seiko won Sports; Louis Vuitton won Artistic Crafts; and independents including Ferdinand Berthoud, Daniel Roth, Otsuka Lotec and Konstantin Chaykin took categories of their own.

Can you buy a GPHG-winning watch?

Most GPHG winners are ultra-high-end or made in tiny numbers by independent makers, so they are rarely available off the shelf — many are allocation- or order-only grails with long waits. A few are more attainable, such as the Grand Seiko Sports winner and Chopard’s Time Only L.U.C. For any of them, an honest read on the real price and availability is the place to start.
