The Story

Saturday night. Budapest. The Puskás Aréna. Two of Europe's most expensively assembled squads face off in the biggest game in club football. But before the first whistle blows, we're watching something else entirely — what's on their wrists.

The Champions League Final has always been the most concentrated gathering of football wealth on the planet. And where there is wealth, there are watches. Not the pre-match tunnel walk. Not the warm-up. The moment the cameras find a player in the dugout, or a manager on the touchline, or a substitute stretching along the sideline — that's when you see what they're actually wearing.

We've tracked the known collections of both squads heading into Saturday. Below is what we know, what we expect, and exactly what to look for when the broadcast goes live.

Saturday Night — Live

What to Watch For When the Cameras Roll

01

The tunnel walk. Both squads line up before kick-off. This is your best window — wrists are visible, cameras linger, and players are stationary. Mbappé and Saka are the two to focus on first.

02

The dugout cutaways. Substitutes and managers. Arteta on the touchline at a UCL Final is appointment viewing — his wrist will be on camera more than most players' faces.

03

Post-match celebrations. The moment the final whistle goes, watches come back on. This is where you see the real pieces — the ones too valuable to risk on the bench.

04

The trophy lift. If Arsenal win — watch Ødegaard's wrist as he raises the Champions League trophy. That image will be everywhere by Sunday morning.

05

Bus arrival, pre-match. Both squads arrive separately. Street level shots, players stepping off. This is where Rice, Dembélé, and the squad players surface — Instagram is your best source here in real time.
