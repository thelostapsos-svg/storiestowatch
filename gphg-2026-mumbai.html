On 28 September 2026, the Grand Prix d’Horlogerie de Genève — the prize the watch world treats as its Oscars — opened its 2026 exhibition not in Geneva, Paris or London, but in Mumbai. For four days, at The St. Regis, the nominees for watchmaking’s highest honour sat under glass in India, hosted by Ethos, the country’s largest luxury-watch retailer. We’re covering an exhibition rather than a watch because the venue is the story: where the GPHG chooses to show its nominees is a statement about where the watch world is looking next.

What happened

The GPHG nominees, in the metal — in Mumbai

A travelling exhibition of the year’s most exceptional watches, on Indian soil.

Each year the GPHG shortlists the finest watchmaking on earth — this cycle, 84 timepieces across 14 categories, drawn from 320 watches entered by 194 brands (the full field is in our GPHG 2026 nominees guide). Before the winners are named, the nominees tour the world so collectors can see them up close, and from 28 September to 1 October that tour came to The St. Regis Mumbai, hosted by Ethos with hands-on viewings, workshops and expert-led tours (The Hour Markers).

It is not the first time the GPHG exhibition has reached India, but the 2026 edition arrives at a different moment — with India no longer a courtesy stop on the calendar, but one of the markets the industry is actively courting (WatchTime India). For the Indian enthusiast, it meant something rare: the chance to stand in front of a tourbillon, an astronomical complication or an artistic-crafts dial that most people only ever see on a screen.

Why it matters

India takes its seat at the top table

A quiet signal, worth reading closely.

The GPHG could hold its exhibition anywhere. That it brought the nominees to Mumbai says the obvious thing out loud: India is now one of the fastest-growing luxury-watch markets in the world, and the industry’s most prestigious body wants to be seen courting it. GPHG Foundation president Raymond Loretan pointed to the country’s “increasingly significant role” in the watch world — diplomatic language for a real shift in where the next generation of collectors is being made (ANI / ThePrint).

You can see the same current everywhere this year: global maisons signing Indian ambassadors, Jacob & Co. building a watch around Lord Shiva for the Indian market, and Titan designing its own in-house mechanical movement. A GPHG exhibition in Mumbai is the high end of that same story — the recognition, from Geneva itself, that India isn’t just buying watches now; it’s being taken seriously as a watch culture.

Questions, answered

The quick answers

What is the GPHG?

The Grand Prix d’Horlogerie de Genève is the watch industry’s most prestigious annual awards, often called the “Oscars of watchmaking.” Each year an academy shortlists the finest timepieces across 14 categories and crowns winners in Geneva, led by the top “Aiguille d’Or” Grand Prix.

When and where was the GPHG 2026 exhibition in Mumbai?

It ran from 28 September to 1 October 2026 at The St. Regis Mumbai, hosted by Ethos, India’s largest luxury-watch retailer, with public viewings of the nominated watches plus workshops and expert-led tours. It was one stop on the GPHG’s 2026 world tour of nominees.

Why does the GPHG coming to India matter?

The GPHG shows its nominees in the markets it considers most important. Bringing the 2026 exhibition to Mumbai is a public signal that India is now one of the world’s fastest-growing luxury-watch markets and is being taken seriously as a watch culture, not just a place that buys watches.

When are the GPHG 2026 winners announced?

The winners are revealed at the awards ceremony in Geneva on 7 November 2026. The full shortlist of 84 nominees across 14 categories is in our GPHG 2026 nominees guide.
