The Object of Desire · No. 003

Australia’s Most Expensive Watch Is One Nobody Could Buy

A gem-set Rolex Daytona you can’t purchase at retail sold for AU$612,000 — the most expensive watch ever auctioned in Australia. And the man who set the last record is the one who sold this one.

The Object of Desire · No. 003 · Rolex Daytona ‘Rainbow’ · Ref. 126598RBOW

By Mathew · Founder, Stories To Watch · 31 July 2026

A Rolex Cosmograph Daytona ‘Rainbow’ (Ref. 126598RBOW) sold through First State Auctions in Sydney for AU$612,000 — the most expensive watch ever sold at auction in Australia. The hammer fell on a Sunday night, on a Rolex that most of the people who wanted it were never allowed to buy. This is The Object of Desire, No. 003 — and unlike the two before it, its legend isn’t history or design. It’s pure want.

The Crash (No. 001) was a design legend — a watch whose melted case became art. The Moonwatch (No. 002) was a history legend — a chronograph that timed humanity’s first steps on another world. The Rainbow Daytona is neither. It is not the best Rolex. It did not go anywhere or do anything. It is simply the one people want most and can least have — and that, it turns out, is worth a country’s record.

The Grail You Are Not Allowed To Buy

Rolex lists the current Rainbow Daytona (Ref. 126598RBOW) at roughly AU$324,700 — and still will not sell most people one. The Rainbow is allocation-only, so that retail figure is almost theoretical: Rolex releases the watch only to a handful of its most valued, longest-standing clients. The one you technically “can” buy for AU$324,700 is one you will almost never be offered. Demand outruns supply by an order of magnitude, and the gap between the two is where the real price lives.

What you are buying, when you finally can, is spectacle. The Ref. 126598RBOW is 18-carat gold, its bezel set with a graduated arc of 36 baguette-cut sapphires that runs the full spectrum like a prism, its lugs studded with diamonds, its black dial marked by sapphire hour indexes. First launched in 2012 (Ref. 116595RBOW), it was reintroduced in 2024 with Rolex’s in-house Calibre 4131. It is loud, it is expensive, and it is deliberately, structurally scarce. That combination — not craft, not provenance — is the entire engine of its value.

The Record — And The Twist

The Rainbow hammered at AU$510,000; with First State’s 20% buyer’s premium, the winning bidder committed to AU$612,000. That cleared the previous Australian auction record — AU$450,000, set in late 2023 by a 1946 Patek Philippe reference 1436 through the same Sydney house — by AU$162,000, drawing around 60 bids from Australia, Singapore and the United States before going to an anonymous US investor (Forbes Australia). And here is the part that turns a sale into a story: the collector who set that old record — the man who paid AU$450,000 for the Patek in 2023 — is the same anonymous Australian tech founder who consigned the Rainbow that broke it. He set the benchmark by buying and broke it by selling. The Rainbow headlined his 28-watch collection — Rolex, Audemars Piguet, Richard Mille, worth an estimated AU$2 million and billed by the house as the largest single-owner Swiss watch collection ever offered in the country (Jeweller).

You cannot buy this watch. You can only outbid the entire world for one — and someone paid a national record to do exactly that.

A Sydney Room Beat Geneva

First State says this was the first example of the reference (126598RBOW) offered at auction anywhere in the world — a saleroom in Sydney got to it before Geneva, New York or Hong Kong, the cities that have owned watch records for a generation (DMARGE). That is the second, quieter record inside the first. Before it, Australia’s watch-auction ceiling had been set by a Patek, and before that a Paul Newman Daytona at Sotheby’s — a famously narrow pool. The Rainbow blew the roof off it.

That geography matters more than it looks. The record wasn’t set by an old-money European collector in a Geneva ballroom; it was set by new tech wealth, online, bid on from three countries, and won by an American buying an Australian consignment of a Swiss watch. The grail market has gone borderless — and the money chasing the very top of it is younger, more global, and less patient than the market that came before it. When a watch is defined by scarcity, that is exactly the kind of money that pays the most for it.

What This Says About Desire

Stand the three Objects of Desire side by side and the Rainbow is the honest one. The Crash asks you to love a shape; the Moonwatch asks you to love a story. The Rainbow asks for nothing but the wanting itself. Its value is not in what it does or where it’s been — a steel Daytona keeps better time relative to its price, and a vintage one carries more history. The Rainbow’s worth is the plain, uncomfortable fact that almost nobody can have it, and a great many people would like to.

That is not a criticism. It is simply the clearest example in modern watches of a truth the whole category runs on: at the top, you are rarely paying for the object. You are paying for permission — the right to hold what others are not allowed to. The Rainbow just wears that truth in gold and sapphire, out in the open, where a record could find it.

The Rainbow — Your Questions

How much did the Rolex Daytona Rainbow sell for?

A Rolex Cosmograph Daytona ‘Rainbow’ (Ref. 126598RBOW) sold for AU$612,000 through First State Auctions in Sydney in July 2026 — a hammer price of AU$510,000 plus a 20% buyer’s premium. It is the most expensive watch ever sold at auction in Australia, and it went to an anonymous US investor. All figures are in Australian dollars.

What is the Rolex Daytona Rainbow?

It is Rolex’s most flamboyant Cosmograph Daytona: a gold chronograph with a bezel set with a graduated spectrum of baguette-cut sapphires and diamond-set lugs. First launched in 2012 (Ref. 116595RBOW), the current generation (Ref. 126598RBOW) arrived in 2024 with the in-house Calibre 4131. It is one of the most coveted modern Rolexes and trades well into six figures.

Why can’t you buy a Rolex Daytona Rainbow?

It is allocation-only. The Rainbow carries a recommended retail of roughly AU$324,700, but Rolex reserves it for its most valued long-standing clients, so you cannot simply buy one. Because demand vastly outstrips the tiny supply, examples change hands on the secondary market and at auction for well above retail.

What was the previous most expensive watch sold at auction in Australia?

The previous record was AU$450,000, set in late 2023 by a 1946 Patek Philippe reference 1436 chronograph, sold through the same house, First State Auctions in Sydney. The collector who bought that record-setting Patek is the same anonymous Australian tech founder who later consigned the Rainbow Daytona that broke the record.

How much does a Rolex Daytona Rainbow cost?

The current yellow-gold Rainbow (Ref. 126598RBOW) has a recommended retail of about AU$324,700, but because it is allocation-only it rarely sells at that figure. On the secondary market and at auction it commands a significant premium — the July 2026 First State example reached AU$612,000 including buyer’s premium.

What is the most expensive watch ever sold at auction in Australia?

The most expensive watch ever sold at auction in Australia is a Rolex Cosmograph Daytona ‘Rainbow’ (Ref. 126598RBOW), which sold for AU$612,000 through First State Auctions in Sydney in July 2026 — a hammer price of AU$510,000 plus a 20% buyer’s premium. It broke the previous record of AU$450,000, held since 2023 by a 1946 Patek Philippe.

The Honest Takeaway

The AU$612,000 Rainbow Daytona is a watch you will almost certainly never be offered — and if you were, its ~AU$324,700 sticker is not the number you’d pay. But the lesson under this record is one you can use on any watch you’ll ever consider: with the pieces at the very top, scarcity is the product. Understand which part of a watch’s price is craft, which is history, and which is simply the fact that you can’t easily get one — and you’ll always know exactly what you’re buying, and whether it’s worth it to you.
