On 16 September 2026, Jacob & Co. unveiled the Epic X Mahadev Edition — the first time the New York high-watchmaking house has built a watch around Lord Shiva (Esquire India). It is a 44mm titanium skeleton on the brand’s sporty Epic X platform, and its openworked dial is a small altar: the trishul, the damru, the sacred Om, and the words “Om Namah Shivaya” at six o’clock. When the lights go down, blue Super-LumiNova sets it glowing. It is also, tellingly, sold in one market only — India, through a single retailer. This is exactly the kind of story-first, culturally specific grail this market has learned to chase, and we read it the same way we read the auction rooms — against where the money is actually going in 2026.

What it is

A god on a skeleton dial

Not a print, not an engraving on the back — the deity is the dial.

The Epic X Mahadev Edition takes Jacob & Co.’s open, architectural Epic X and turns its skeletonised centre into a devotional scene. The figure of Lord Shiva sits at the heart of the movement, surrounded by the symbols most closely associated with him: the trishul (trident), the damru (the hourglass drum), and the sacred Om, with the mantra “Om Namah Shivaya” inscribed at the six o’clock position (Free Press Journal).

In the brand’s own reading, the trident stands for control over past, present and future, while the damru carries the flow of cosmic energy — the same rhythm, the marketing suggests, as a beating movement. It is the first time Jacob & Co. has dedicated a watch to Shiva, and it lands as the most overtly devotional piece in a growing line of India-inspired Epic X editions.

The detail that sells it

By day, a sculpture. After dark, an awakening.

This is the trick the photos can’t quite hold: the watch has two faces.

In daylight the dial reads as fine, three-dimensional metalwork — a small sculpted panorama you have to lean in to decode. Then the lights drop, and Jacob & Co.’s blue Super-LumiNova takes over: Shiva and the surrounding symbols glow, and the composition transforms into what the house frames as divine energy and spiritual awakening (Esquire India).

It is a genuinely clever piece of theatre. A watch that is one object on the wrist at dinner and another entirely in a dark room is doing exactly what an object of desire is supposed to do — giving its owner a moment no one else in the room can see.

Price & availability

You can only buy it in one country

India-exclusive, one retailer, and a price the brand hasn’t published.

Here is the part that makes the Mahadev unusual even for Jacob & Co.: it is available exclusively through Ethos Watches in India (Esquire India). There is no global boutique roll-out; the piece was made for one market and sold through one partner.

On price, be careful with the numbers floating around. Jacob & Co. did not publish an official global figure at launch — one major report noted the price was simply not public (Free Press Journal), while Indian coverage has reported it at around ₹35.88 lakh (Esquire India). Treat that as an indicative figure, not a confirmed sticker: with an India-only, retailer-exclusive piece like this, the only number that counts is the one Ethos quotes you directly.

Why it matters

The market is buying stories, not movements

A god on the dial is a very literal version of a much bigger shift.

Step back and the Mahadev fits a pattern we keep tracing. The watches that move people — and prices — in 2026 are the ones carrying a story you can retell: scarcity, culture, provenance, meaning. It is the same force that let a rare quartz Cartier Cheich become the most valuable Cartier ever sold, and the same one behind the split running through the 2026 market, where money has stopped chasing badges and started chasing narrative.

A watch built explicitly around a deity, sold in a single devotional market, is that logic at its most undiluted. Whether the Mahadev becomes collectible or stays a curiosity will come down to how few were made and how the market reads it — but the instinct behind it, that meaning now sells harder than mechanics, is the truest thing about it.

Questions, answered

The quick answers

What is the Jacob & Co. Mahadev watch?

The Jacob & Co. Epic X Mahadev Edition is a 44mm titanium skeleton watch, unveiled on 16 September 2026, built as the house’s first tribute to Lord Shiva. Its openworked dial features the figure of Shiva along with the trishul, damru and Om symbols, and the mantra “Om Namah Shivaya” at six o’clock, with blue Super-LumiNova that glows in the dark.

How much does the Jacob & Co. Mahadev Edition cost?

Jacob & Co. did not publish an official global price at launch. Indian coverage has reported a figure of around ₹35.88 lakh, but this should be treated as indicative. Because the watch is sold exclusively through Ethos Watches in India, the reliable price is the one the retailer quotes directly on request.

Where can you buy the Mahadev Edition?

The Epic X Mahadev Edition is available exclusively through Ethos Watches in India. There was no global boutique release at launch — the watch was created for the Indian market and sold through a single retail partner.

What movement does the Mahadev Edition use?

It uses Jacob & Co.’s JCAM02, a hand-wound skeleton calibre running at 28,800 vibrations per hour (4 Hz) with a 48-hour power reserve, housed in a 44mm titanium case. Full technical details had not been published by the brand at the time of writing.
