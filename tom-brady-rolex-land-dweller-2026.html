On a Fox NFL broadcast in late October, Tom Brady did what Tom Brady now does every game day: he flashed a watch. This one was a platinum Rolex Land-Dweller — the 40mm Ref. 127336, with a honeycomb ice-blue dial, worn on its flat Jubilee bracelet and posted to his Instagram before kickoff (Robb Report). The internet clocked the price — about $68,400 at Rolex retail, though a brand-new, allocation-only platinum Rolex like this really changes hands nearer $88,000. But the real story is smaller than either number and hiding in plain sight: the colour of the dial. In Rolex’s world, that one detail tells you everything.

What happened

Another game, another grail

Brady’s broadcast-day watch flex is now its own sport.

Brady wore the platinum Land-Dweller while calling a game at Atlanta’s Mercedes-Benz Stadium, sharing it to his Instagram Stories pre-kickoff — part of a weekly ritual that has turned his wrist into must-watch content for collectors. It isn’t a one-off, either: earlier in October he was seen in an orange-sapphire Rolex Daytona reported at around $300,000, and a gem-set “Rainbow” Daytona reportedly worth some $500,000 (AOL). Against that company, a $68,000 platinum Rolex is almost the restrained choice.

But it’s arguably the most interesting of the three — because it’s Rolex’s newest and most talked-about watch, and because of that dial.

The tell

Ice-blue means one thing: platinum

Rolex reserves this exact colour for its most precious metal — nothing else.

Here is the collector’s shortcut worth knowing. Rolex makes its ice-blue dial colour available only on platinum watches — it is a quiet, deliberate signal of the metal, used across the Day-Date, the Daytona and now the Land-Dweller. So the instant you spot that pale, frosty blue, you can skip the guessing: it is not steel, not white gold. It is platinum — the heaviest, rarest and most expensive metal Rolex works in.

On Brady’s watch the dial carries a honeycomb motif unique to the Land-Dweller, which catches the light in facets. Beautiful — but the colour is the code. Next time a celebrity “Rolex” flashes across your screen, look at the dial first: ice-blue, and you’re looking at the top of the range.

The watch

The Land-Dweller: Rolex’s biggest swing in 13 years

An all-new collection, an all-new movement — not a facelift.

The Land-Dweller, launched in 2025, is Rolex’s first entirely new watch collection in 13 years — a genuine event for a brand that usually evolves by millimetres. It is an integrated-bracelet sports watch: a fluted bezel, that textured honeycomb dial, and a flat Jubilee bracelet that flows straight out of the case. Under it sits a brand-new engine, the Calibre 7135, running at a fast 5 Hz with Rolex’s new high-efficiency Dynapulse silicon escapement and roughly a 66-hour power reserve (Monochrome).

It comes in steel, Everose and white gold as well as platinum, in 36mm and 40mm — but only the platinum versions get Brady’s ice-blue dial. It is, in short, exactly the kind of brand-new, hard-to-get Rolex the whole market is chasing right now.

Why is the Rolex dial ice-blue?

Rolex reserves its ice-blue dial colour exclusively for watches made of platinum. It is a deliberate signal of the metal, so an ice-blue dial tells you the watch is platinum — the rarest and most expensive metal in Rolex’s range — rather than steel or white gold.
