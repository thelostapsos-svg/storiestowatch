The Myth · ΧΡΟΝΟΣ

Before the first clock, there was a god. The Greeks called him Chronos — Time itself — coiled around the world like a serpent, devouring all he fathered. Gold tarnishes, empires fall; the hour, once spent, never returns.

Mortals could not stop him — so they learned to carry a piece of him. The watch: the only way a man has ever held time in his hand — not a measure of the hour, but a relic of defiance, proof that for one lifetime, you kept pace with a god.

Beyond the Hours

The lesser keepers.

Chronos was never alone. The Greeks gave time many hands.

Kairos

ΚΑΙΡΟΣ

The fleeting, perfect moment — the time you seize, not the time you count. Chronos numbers the hours; Kairos chooses the one that matters.

Aion

ΑΙΩΝ

Eternity — the wheel with no beginning and no end, the time of gods, not of mortals. Where Chronos devours, Aion simply turns.

The Horae

ΩΡΑΙ

The Hours themselves, daughters of order — keepers of the seasons, openers of heaven's gates. Every dial made since is their inheritance.
