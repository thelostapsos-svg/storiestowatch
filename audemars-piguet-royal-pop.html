In September 2026, Audemars Piguet told CNBC that its “Royal Pop” collaboration with Swatch is breaking sales and engagement records — a rare bright spot as the wider luxury-watch market softens and new US tariffs bite. That’s a striking headline for a $400 plastic-cased pocket watch. But the number that actually matters to anyone buying a real Royal Oak isn’t the hype — it’s what a viral $400 taste of the design did to the $30,000-plus watch it borrows from. This is the market as we read it, week to week, across the 75 verified dealers in our Watch Finder.

What it is

A $400 Royal Oak you keep in your pocket

The first time in over 50 years that Audemars Piguet has let the Royal Oak design out of the house.

The Swatch × Audemars Piguet “Royal Pop” launched globally on 16 May 2026: a collection of eight Swiss-made Bioceramic pocket watches that reinterpret the Royal Oak’s octagonal codes through the modular, colourful spirit of Swatch’s 1980s POP line. Six “Lépine” models are priced at $400 and two “Savonnette” versions at $420, each powered by a hand-wound version of Swatch’s SISTEM51 movement (WatchTime; Swatch).

What makes it genuinely historic isn’t the format — it’s the licence. This is the first time Audemars Piguet has allowed the Royal Oak design language to be produced externally in more than five decades, since Gerald Genta drew it in 1972. It sold out within minutes in many locations, and Audemars Piguet says it is donating its proceeds to fund watchmaker training (Audemars Piguet). Whatever you think of a plastic Royal Oak, it did exactly what the MoonSwatch did for Omega: put an untouchable name in a normal person’s hand.

The fresh news

“Breaking records” — against the tide

A viral hit in a year when expensive Swiss watches are getting harder to sell.

The reason this is back in the headlines four months after launch is the result. In late September 2026, Audemars Piguet said the Royal Pop was breaking sales and engagement records, generating billions of social impressions and defying a broader luxury-watch slowdown — and doing it while the US has layered fresh tariffs onto Swiss goods (CNBC, 26 September 2026).

For a house that guards its exclusivity as tightly as Audemars Piguet, that’s a deliberate bet: trade a little scarcity mystique for enormous reach and a new generation of people who now recognise — and covet — the octagon. It is marketing on a scale no advertising budget can buy. The question every existing Royal Oak owner quietly asked, though, was simpler: does flooding the world with $400 Royal Oaks cheapen mine?

For $400, anyone can hold the shape of a Royal Oak. What that shape is worth on a real wrist is a completely different question.

What it did to the real market

A blip, not a collapse

The launch made noise. The secondary market barely flinched — then moved on.

Here is the concrete data point. In the week of the chaotic Royal Pop launch, the average secondary-market price of an Audemars Piguet Royal Oak (across references) slipped from roughly $67,100 to about $65,100 — a dip of around 3% (WatchPro, May 2026). A wobble, in other words — the sort of short-term softness that a flood of attention and a few nervous sellers can cause, not a structural repricing.

What it means for you

The $400 is the taste. The Royal Oak is the meal.

A fun collectible is not a store of value — and the two shouldn’t be confused.

If you snagged a Royal Pop for $400, enjoy it — it’s a genuinely charming object and a piece of watch history. Just don’t mistake it for an investment: it’s a mass-produced Swatch, and the ones flipping above retail today are riding hype, not scarcity. The real store of value is the watch it points at.

Questions, answered

The quick answers

What is the Audemars Piguet Royal Pop?

The Royal Pop is a Swatch × Audemars Piguet collaboration launched on 16 May 2026 — a collection of eight Bioceramic pocket watches, priced from $400, that reinterpret the Royal Oak design in Swatch’s colourful POP style. It is the first time Audemars Piguet has licensed the Royal Oak design language for external production in more than 50 years, and it is powered by a hand-wound version of Swatch’s SISTEM51 movement.

How much does the Royal Pop cost?

Retail is $400 for the six “Lépine” models and $420 for the two “Savonnette” versions, sold through Swatch. It sold out within minutes at many locations, so pieces have appeared on the resale market above retail — but that premium reflects launch hype rather than scarcity, and should not be confused with the value of a genuine Royal Oak.

Did the Royal Pop affect real Royal Oak prices?

Only briefly. In the week of the launch, the average secondary-market price of an Audemars Piguet Royal Oak (across references) dipped roughly 3% — from about $67,100 to around $65,100, per WatchPro (May 2026). That is short-term noise; the value of a genuine Royal Oak rests on scarcity, finishing and provenance, which a $400 licensed pocket watch does not change.

Is the Royal Pop a real Audemars Piguet?

It is a genuine, official Audemars Piguet × Swatch collaboration — but it is a Swatch-made Bioceramic pocket watch at $400, not a Manufacture Royal Oak wristwatch (which starts around $31,900 at retail and trades far higher pre-owned). Think of it as a licensed taste of the design, not the grail itself.
