Some watches are rare. This one was effectively a rumour. One of only seven Patek Philippe Ref. 3452 Dual Time wristwatches ever made — and the last of the seven to surface — has emerged from a single family that quietly held it for 65 years, and will cross the block at Christie’s in New York on 4 December 2026, estimated at $800,000 to $1.6 million. It is the first Ref. 3452 offered at auction since 2006. And it isn’t just old and scarce: it’s a direct ancestor of the travel watches Patek still builds today — the ones you actually can buy.

What happened

A watch the market had never seen

Hidden in one family since 1961 — now surfacing for the very first time.

Christie’s is offering the Dual Time Ref. 3452/1 as a highlight of its December Luxury Week, in the Important Watches sale in New York on 4 December 2026 (Christie’s, via Artdaily). The example on offer was crafted in 1961 and, per Christie’s, acquired by its first owner that same year — then kept within the family ever since. Sixty-five years later, it reaches the open market for the first time.

That phrase — fresh to market — is the rarest thing in vintage Patek. A watch with no prior sale, no circulation and an unbroken family line carries no questions about swapped parts or restoration. Here it is multiplied by sheer scarcity: Christie’s says only seven examples of the reference were ever made — two in 1960, two in 1961 and the final three by 1963 — and this is the last of them to appear.

The watch

Patek’s first travel watch

Before the Aquanaut Travel Time, before the Calatrava Pilot — there was the 3452.

The 3452 traces to a 1954 prototype by Louis Cottier, the independent watchmaker whose complications gave Patek its world-time and travel watches — the man behind the legendary Heure Universelle world-timers. The 3452 was among Patek’s earliest wristwatches built to show two time zones at once — an innovative twin-dial layout, a true dual-time complication for the traveller, decades before that became a genre. This example runs a modified Caliber 27-460, is cased in 18-carat white and yellow gold, and wears an integrated two-tone gold bracelet.

For context on value: when a yellow-gold 3452/1 last changed hands — at Sotheby’s Geneva in 2006 — it sold for CHF 240,000. Twenty years on, Christie’s seven-figure estimate reflects how far the market for the rarest vintage Patek complications has run — and how little ever actually comes up.

A watch the collecting world had only heard whispers of, built for a world of two time zones — surfacing after a lifetime, to decide what a legend is worth.

What it means for you

You can’t buy the 3452. You can buy its heirs.

Patek still makes travel watches — and these are genuinely attainable.

Nobody reading this is buying a $1.6-million, one-of-seven grail — and that’s fine, because the 3452’s whole idea lives on in Patek’s current collection. The modern travel-time Pateks are the direct descendants of that 1961 watch: same purpose — two time zones, elegantly — at a fraction of the drama. They’re still serious Pateks, some carry waitlists, but unlike the 3452 you can actually pursue one.

Questions, answered

The quick answers

What is the Patek Philippe Ref. 3452/1?

It is one of Patek Philippe’s earliest dual-time (travel) wristwatches, developed from a 1954 prototype by the complication specialist Louis Cottier and powered by a modified Caliber 27-460. Only seven examples of the Ref. 3452 were ever made; the one at Christie’s is an 18-carat white-and-yellow-gold example crafted in 1961.

How much is the lost Patek 3452/1 expected to sell for?

Christie’s estimates the watch at $800,000 to $1.6 million. It will be offered in the Important Watches sale in New York on 4 December 2026, as a highlight of Christie’s December Luxury Week. It is the first Ref. 3452 to come to auction since 2006.

Why is this Patek so rare?

Only seven Ref. 3452 watches were ever made — two in 1960, two in 1961 and the final three by 1963. This particular example stayed with its original owner’s family for 65 years and has never before been offered for sale, making it the last of the seven to surface.

Which Patek travel watches can you actually buy today?

Patek’s modern travel-time line is the attainable heir to the 3452: the Aquanaut Travel Time (Ref. 5164) and the Calatrava Pilot Travel Time (Refs. 5524 and 5224R) both show two time zones. They are premium watches and some carry waitlists, but unlike the one-of-seven 3452/1 they are part of the current collection.
