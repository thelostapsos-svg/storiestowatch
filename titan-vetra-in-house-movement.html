On 18 September 2026, Titan launched Vetra — what the company bills as India’s first in-house mechanical movement — and with it a new Titan Automatics collection of four watches priced from ₹47,995 (Deccan Herald). We rarely cover watches at this price. We’re covering this one because it isn’t really about a ₹50,000 watch — it’s about a country learning to build the one part everyone else imports, and it happens in the market our readers care about most.

What it is

A movement, made at home

The name is the thesis: Veda, knowledge; Tra, the instrument that channels it.

Vetra is Titan’s first branded in-house mechanical movement — the 7A series, a single architecture flexible enough to carry different complications. By Titan’s account it runs around 130 components, roughly 90% of them made in-house, with 23 jewels, a 3 Hz (21,600 vph) beat and up to a 72-hour power reserve — three full days on a single wind (WatchTime India).

That last phrase — made in-house — is the whole story. India has built watches for over forty years, but on movements designed and supplied by others. Designing and manufacturing the calibre itself is the hard, expensive, prestige part of watchmaking that only a handful of countries do at scale. Titan crossing that line is less a product launch than a national milestone, and it deserves to be read as one.

Anyone can assemble a watch. Building the movement that runs it is the line most countries never cross — and India just did.

Why it matters

Not a cheaper Switzerland — something of its own

The ambition on show is bigger than the sticker price.

Titan isn’t pretending Vetra rivals a Swiss haute-horlogerie calibre, and it shouldn’t. What it represents is capability — and confidence. As Titan’s watches chief Rahul Shukla put it, the company isn’t trying to build “a higher Swiss Alps,” but something “uniquely ours” (The Hour Markers). That framing matters in a country of 1.4 billion people where mechanical watches are having a genuine renaissance among younger buyers rediscovering the romance of a movement you wind.

There’s a commercial engine under the sentiment, too: Titan expects its mechanical-watch business to grow around 30% this year toward roughly ₹350 crore (Indian Retailer). For the world’s most populous market, an accessible, home-built automatic is exactly how you turn a nation of quartz wearers into a nation of mechanical enthusiasts — the same appetite that, one tier up, drives the grail market we track every week in the 2026 market read.

What it means for you

A gateway, not a grail — and that’s the point

Where the Vetra sits, and where it leads.

Be clear-eyed about what a ₹48,000–55,000 Titan is and isn’t. It is a genuine, in-house automatic with real specs and a real story — an excellent first mechanical watch, or a proud everyday piece. It is not a store of value or a substitute for a $30,000-plus grail, and you shouldn’t buy it expecting either.

But that’s exactly why it matters to a reader here. The Vetra is the on-ramp. The same instinct that makes you love a watch you have to wind is the one that, a few years later, has you reading about a Royal Oak, a Nautilus or a Daytona — and wondering what one actually costs and where to find it. Questions, answered

The quick answers

What is Titan Vetra?

Vetra is Titan’s first in-house mechanical movement, launched on 18 September 2026 — the 7A series, with around 130 components (roughly 90% made in-house by Titan), 23 jewels, a 3 Hz beat and up to a 72-hour power reserve. It powers the new Titan Automatics collection and is billed as India’s first in-house mechanical movement. The name blends “Veda” (knowledge) and “Tra” (instrument).

How much do the Titan Automatics cost?

The collection is priced from ₹47,995 to ₹54,995. There are four models — Infinium, Dualis (GMT), Midnite (big date) and Atrium (offset seconds, with an open-heart option) — all in 316L steel with a sapphire crystal, sold through Titan World, Helios and titan.co.in.

Is Vetra really India’s first in-house movement?

Titan positions it as India’s first branded in-house mechanical movement, and reports that about 90% of its roughly 130 parts are made in-house. India has assembled watches for decades, but generally on movements supplied by others — designing and manufacturing the calibre itself is the milestone here, and it is a genuine step-change for Indian watchmaking.

Is a Titan Vetra a good investment?

No — and that isn’t its job. At around ₹48,000–55,000 it’s an accessible, well-made first automatic with a real in-house movement, not a store of value like a scarce Swiss grail. Buy it to enjoy and to start with mechanical watchmaking, not as an appreciating asset.
